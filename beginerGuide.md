Overview
The 

Seer
 draws strength from their faith in the return of the gods, using prayers and divine arts to heal, protect, and support allies. Seers provide versatile support and control, though they can struggle when overwhelmed or locked down.

This class is the closest of all the Mistfall Hunter classes to a cleric, and features two distinct and unique build paths:

Reverent allows for a support playstyle, allowing the 

Seer
 to heal allies and place supportive runes using the Catalyst weapon.
Blasphemer uses a Mace and has a disruptive offensive playstyle, which is the better Solo option for the 

Seer
.

Strengths And Weaknesses
The 

Seer
 has a unique identity as the support and control Class in Mistfall Hunter. Its specific strengths and weaknesses depend on which path you choose (Reverent or Blasphemer).

Whichever path you choose, you'll need to make the most out of your unique Skills, allowing to save teammates and disrupt enemies alike. The 

Seer
 excels at controlling the battlefield and tiding the course of PvP encounters.

Rewarding to learn. The Seer is great in most Trio setups, making it a solid class choice in multiplayer.
Potent Skills that can turn the tide of battle if used at the right time
Reverent is the only healing playstyle in the game, and a great addition to most Trio teams.
Blasphemer has access to drain mechanics and has tools to catch kiters. Excels in burst windows.
Not recommended for beginners. The Seer has a unique playstyle requiring more setup and Skill use than other classes.
Can't switch between Reverent and Blasphemer in a match; must choose beforehand.
Reverent path depends on teammates to protect them, due to their low damage and lack of defensive tools.
Blasphemer can be hard to use due to their long cooldowns and niche Skills.
Reverent Skills
The Reverent 

Seer
 Skills are divided into two categories: Runes and Divine Arts. 

You can equip up to 3 runes. During a game, selecting a Rune modifies the effects of 

Rune Summon
.  Divine Arts function like regular Skills, each granting different effects. Some runes and divine arts are mutually exclusive, allowing only one to be equipped at a time. 

Psionic Orb




Psionic Orb
 is the Reverent 

Seer
's light attack. Firing and hitting 

Psionic Orb
 accelerates Psionic Energy recharge. (Psionic Energy is needed to use 

Rune Summon
)


Psionic Orb
 can be charged by holding down the button, dealing higher Damage, Impact, and Range.

Rune Summon




Rune Summon
 creates a Rune Pillar at the currently selected rune at the target location. It requires Psionic Energy to be used, which recharges over time and when using 

Psionic Orb
.

The Rune Pillar is considered a Construct: It has a limited uptime and can be attacked and destroyed by enemy attacks.

There are various runes that modify 

Rune Summon
, granting it different effects:

Punishment Rune

Punishment Rune
 continuously attacks the lowest HP enemy in range, prioritizing enemy players over monsters. It is used as a main damaging tool.

Healing Rune
Unlocks at Level 3


Healing Rune
 recovers Health for all allies in range. It has limited uses per match. 

Shelter Rune
Unlocks at Level 3


Shelter Rune
 shields all allies in range, making it useful against Area-of-Effect Skills and when initiating fights. The lowest health ally in range receives a continuous Shield as well. 

Stealth Rune
Unlocks at Level 10


Stealth Rune
 grants Stealth to all allies in range, which persists even after taking damage. Useful when fighting in enclosed environments. 

Burst Rune

Burst Rune
 deals magic damage upon impact in a reduced area, and recovers Psionic Energy for each player hit. Enemies hit are marked, dealing further Magic Damage and staggering enemies after a few seconds. It's a great offensive tool, but unreliable to use.

Binding Rune
Unlocks at Level 5

Shortly after summoning, 

Binding Rune
 immobilizes all enemies in range and prevents them from jumping for a time. Restores Psionic Energy for each player immobilized. Great defensive tool against melee threats. 

Intimidation Rune
Unlocks at Level 4


Intimidation Rune
 immediately summons a Rune Pillar in front of the 

Seer
, dealing Magic Damage and knocking back nearby enemies. It is one of its best defensive and peel options, since it deploys instantly and does not consume Psionic Energy on cast. 

Windwalker Rune
Unlocks at Level 7


Windwalker Rune
 increases dodge distance for all allies in range, and reduces dodge distance for all enemies in range. It's situational, but great against Area-of-Effect Skills. 

Divine Arts
Divine Arts function like regular Skills in other Classes, and are activated with the usual Skill buttons.

Wind Surge




Wind Surge
 launches a cyclone in the target direction, dealing Magic Damage and knocking back all enemies hit.  It is a reliable damaging and peeling tool against melee enemies.

Healing Art



Healing Art restores HP to all allies near the 

Seer
. It has limited uses per match.

It functions as an additional Healing option to 

Healing Rune

Healing Art

Psionic Shield




Psionic Shield

Seer

It can store multiple charges, making it a great defensive option among all Divine Arts.

Healing Art

Seer

Blasphemer Skills
The 

Seer
's Blasphemer Skills are divided into three categories: Runes, Divine Arts, and 

Unleash Zeal


You can equip up to 3 runes. During a game, selecting a Rune when Curse Mana is full turns Rune: Heavy Strike into Rune: Enchanted Heavy Strike, granting additional effects based on the Rune currently selected. Divine Arts function like regular Skills, each granting different effects. 


Unleash Zeal

Seer

Leaps forward, dealing Physical Damage to enemies hit. When Curse Mana is full, Rune: Heavy Strike is enhanced with the effects of the Rune currently selected. The Runes available are:

Rune: Drain

Rune: Drain

Withered Knight

Rune: Ankle Cut
 deals Magic Damage and creates a shockwave forward. Targets hit directly by the weapon are Immobilized and dealt Impact. Targets hit by the shockwave take reduced damage and are slowed instead.

Unleash Zeal

Rune: Corruption

Rune: Stun

Divine Arts function like regular Skills in other Classes, and are activated with the usual Skill buttons.

Shapeshift




Shapeshift
 transforms the Blasphemer into a Rune, granting high Movement Speed and preventing stagger. The Blasphemer's next attack will dash him toward the target.

Thorn Sigil




Thorn Sigil

 summons a Sigil at the target location. After a brief delay, it deals Magic Damage and pulls enemies toward the

Unleash Zeal

It is best used after the Curse Mana bar is full, as it will allow the Blasphemer to use its tools for as long as possible until the battle ends.

Reverent Seer: How to Build
The Reverent 

Seer
 path shines in Trios, where it can support allies and debuff enemies alike. Its Rune options offer great build diversity, so don't be afraid to choose whichever runes fit the team best.

Skills
For Divine Arts, we'll take 

Healing Art
 for its strong Healing potential, and 

Wind Surge
 as a reliable knockback and damage source.

As for Runes, all options are useful in the right situation, so the Rune choice depends on which classes you're playing with.

Two Runes that are useful in most situations are 

Shelter Rune
 and 

Binding Rune
:


Shelter Rune
 is best used as a fight starts to maximize Shield value. The Shield deploys instantly, and it continuously keeps Shielding the lowest HP ally in range, as well. It doesn't require the whole team to be near the Rune Pillar, as only one player benefits from the extra Shield.

Binding Rune
, if aimed correctly, guarantees immobilizing enemy players without dashing abilities. It can stop melee teams on the spot, and also regenerates Psionic Energy which allows you to use 

Rune Summon
 more often.
The third Rune choice is heavily dependent on your team composition. Some generally good choices are:


Intimidation Rune
 when your team lacks peeling and knockback options.

Windwalker Rune
 provides battle mobility to your team, allowing gap closing. Useful with melee teams.
Damaging Runes like 

Punishment Rune
 and 

Burst Rune
 are not great choices since your Talents and Affixes will be focused on enabling your support playstyle.

Talents
Ebb and Flow: Hitting an enemy with a charged 

Psionic Orb
 will heal the lowest HP ally.
Reshape: If the Reverent shield remains for a period of time, it recovers. 
Rigidity: Stuns enemies that leave 

Binding Rune
's range.
Deep Healing: 

Healing Art
 recovers additional HP based on missing health.
Affixes
Reverent affixes should directly help its support playstyle, enhancing Skill casts:


Seamless
: Helps maximize your cooldown uptime.

Eloquence
: Makes your chants faster.

Creation
: Extends the duration of your 

Rune Summon
.

Spirit Shield
: Enhances your 

Shelter Rune
.

Blasphemer Seer: How to Build
The Blasphemer 

Seer
 path has great dueling potential during 

Unleash Zeal
. This build specializes in enabling and enhancing the Zeal state and giving the Blasphemer the best damaging options.

Skills

Shapeshift
 is a must-have, as it can be used as an engage and disengage tool. It allows the Blasphemer to access enemy backlines in an instant.


Thorn Sigil
 is used as a follow-up to 

Shapeshift
, since it immobilizes and slows enemies around the 

Seer
. Its constant Magic Damage will chip away enemy HP if they dare to fight you head-on.

As for Runes, you can take the choices you find most fun to play. A must-have with this loadout is 

Rune: Stun
, since it can be used directly after 

Thorn Sigil
 hits to guarantee a high-damage Stun. 

Rune: Sweep
 is another high-damaging option that also deals Area-of-Effect damage. 

Talents
Talent choices are focused on enhancing 

Unleash Zeal
:

Inexhaustible: Gain Cursed Mana during Zeal.
Obsession: Increases Zeal duration and gain Movement Speed during Zeal. Taking damage reduces Zeal duration.
Infusion: Recovers a large amount of Curse Mana when entering Zeal.
Keen Edge: After finishing 

Shapeshift
 with its signature dash attack, increases Physical and Magic damage for a short time.
Affixes
Blasphemer affixes support its duelist playstyle:

Strife: Maximizes melee attack damage.
Blessing: Extends the duration of Zeal and other buffs.
Fervor: A good choice for melees in general due to increased max health and healing.
Swift: Increases movement speed, allowing you to improve offensive and defensive maneuvering.
Tips and Tricks
Reverent Tips
Rune Pillar Positioning Matters
Optimize the positioning of your 

Rune Summon
 by placing it where it will be most effective, like in the middle of the battlefield or among your teammates.

Think Ahead: Cooldowns and Resources Matter
If a battle is likely to start, you can use 

Shelter Rune
 preemptively to start the battle with an enhanced shield. Likewise, keep your important cooldowns for PvP fights, don't waste them in short PvE encounters. You'll also need to accumulate Psionic Energy ahead of battles to maximize your 

Rune Summon
 casts.

Healing has Limits
Both your 

Healing Art
 and 

Healing Rune
 have a maximum amount of uses per-run. If you find yourself running low on them, let your team know and head to the nearest extraction point. As a Reverent, your strength is heavily reduced without Healing.

Save Stuns for When It Matters Most
Crowd-control Skills like 

Binding Rune
 and 

Wind Surge
 can be used offensively or defensively. Depending on your team composition and the enemy's, it might be more effective to use them for peeling yourself or other teammates.

Aim for Headshots
The Reverent 

Seer
 has low damage output, but you should still use your 

Psionic Orb
 whenever you're not using  your other Skills. Aim for the head to do more damage.

Blasphemer Tips
Your Zeal Is Everything
Your 

Unleash Zeal
 enables your offensive playstyle. Avoid confrontation when it is on cooldown. Likewise, keep an eye out on your Zeal duration: You'll be weak and exposed when it runs out.

Be Creative When Using Shapeshift

Shapeshift
 grants high movement speed, allowing you to dodge enemy Skills as well as gap close. Find the right timing so you can both dodge and reach enemy backlines, and the fight is sure to tide in your team's favor.

Enemies Don't Expect An Offensive Seer
Most players will expect a 

Seer
 to run the supporting Reverent playstyle. You can take advantage of this and catch enemy squishy players off-guard, as they won't be expecting a Blasphemer to get in their face instead.

Solo Guide: Tips and Tricks
By Ryan Gilliam, Zack, Jen Rothery
Updated: Aug 1, 2026 12:22am GMT+8
Like many Extractions Shooters, Mistfall Hunter supports both group and solo play. But the meta differs wildly whether you're playing with a team or with a friends.

In this guide, we're going to walk you through which classes you should check out if you're playing solo, and give you some good tips on how to extract alive when you have nobody else watching your back.

Best Solo Class
Mistfall Hunter Screenshot 2026.07.31 - 03.18.55.33.png

There are six unique classes in Mistfall Hunter, and they all have perfectly viable ability kits that allow for a myriad of different playstyles. Meaning: over everything else, pick the class you like playing most, and you'll find a playstyle that you'll be able to make a build for in both solos and trios.

Shadowstrix
That being said, some picks are a little more obvious than others, like the Shadowstrix. Look, any class that lets you go invisible will always have an edge in solo play. If you're going in just to grab some materials for your camp and get out, the Shadowstrix will allow you to slip your way past enemies and enemy players with ease. And, if you're someone who lives to hunt other players while solo, the Shadowstrix will give you that ambush factor that can completely change the tides of battle in a game like Mistfall Hunter.

Marauder
Now while the Shadowstrix might be the most obvious of the classes, I'll give you another one that's a little less stealthy: Marauder. Here, you specifically want to go for a hammer build, which might sound counterintuitive, but there's a solid reason for this. The hammer's skills on Marauder deal a ton of damage and heavily stagger enemies. This is great for a taking on bigger and badder PvE enemies with relative ease, as you can just combo them before they have a chance to fight back. You can swap to your sword and shield if you get in a player fight, but there's honestly good reason to just keep your hammer and lock your enemy in place for an extended period of time.

Not Recommended: Sorcerer
As for classes to avoid while solo, you probably want to stay away from the Sorcerer until you're very comfortable with them. This is really just because they're very easy prey for Shadowstrix players, which will almost certainly be the most popular solo class. Without a big beefy Marauder or Withered Knight to protect you, you just have more limited options as the Sorcerer than you do with other classes.

Tips for Solo Players
Mistfall Hunter Screenshot 2026.07.31 - 03.19.13.38.png

Here, we're just going to drop you some nice tips to get you started on solo runs, as they can be a little scary at first. But don't worry, once you're through with these tips and a few matches, you'll be running lobbies like a pro.

Don't overstay your welcome: This is just solid advice for Mistfall Hunter regardless of if you're playing solos or trios, but it's especially true when you're alone. If you're looking to complete a specific objective or get some materials, grab them and get out. Maybe complete a quest if it's on your way to the exit, but you really don't want to hang out in the lobby longer than you have to, otherwise you're going to get yourself killed.
Listen for the heartbeat: It's pretty clear already that the stealth class is going to be the best class in solos, but the Shadowstrix has a tell when they're invisible: a heartbeat sound. You can use this to prepare yourself for a fight if you hear one sneaking up to you, or you can use it to chase one that's trying to run away from a duel.
Ambushes are king in solos: It's pretty hard to hide your entire crew as a trio, but it's pretty easy to slink around the map when you're alone (even if you can't go invisible). If you hear a nearby player and you want to get into a fight, position yourself around a door or over a bridge and try to get the jump on them. Without other teammates to distract you, duels can end very quickly in solos, so those first few hits make a massive difference.
Third parties are even better when you're alone: Cleaning up the tail end of another group's battle is the bread and butter of any winning team in Mistfall Hunter, but it's even easier to do in solos. Because your enemies have nobody else to watch their back, you can easily watch a dangerous duel from the sidelines and then pounce while they're looting to get that ambush advantage we mentioned above.
Doors are much more dangerous as a solo: Managing doors is hugely important in Mistfall Hunter, and you're able to open or close some pretty risky doors when you're playing with friends. As a solo, however, doors will really harm your ability to move around the map. If there's a powerful enemy guarding a door, you can't just have your Marauder kite it to the other side of the area while you open it. You need to kill it or find another way around. Just keep in mind that everything has a cost in an Extraction Shooter, so taking the time to kill a door guardian might make just enough noise to attract another player.
Decide early if you're a solos or trios player: The wonderful thing about Mistfall Hunter is that you can waffle between solos and trios as much as you want. However, you do want to decide how you're going to be playing the game most often. Because Mistfall Hunter is such a resource heavy game, you want to spend your best gear or consumables on the mode where you're likely to make the most progress. So if you're mostly a trios player, don't drop into solos with that amazing trios gear; save it for the next time your friends are on. On the flip side, if you're just hanging with your pals and not taking the game as serious as you might when you're alone, maybe opt for the free ale instead of spending your precious resources.
