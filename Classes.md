Overview
There are currently six playable classes in Mistfall Hunter, each offering distinct playstyles and unique skill expression. 

As a dark fantasy, you'll be able to find your usual tropes ranging from warriors to wizards, but there are some surprises you might not expect from their abilities.

In this guide we'll cover their main playstyles, weapons, and more to help you choose your starting class.

How to Change Classes in Mistfall Hunter
If you end up wanting to try a new class after the tutorial, you'll need to wait until you do the next quest after the tutorial. Upon finishing it, you'll get the option to change classes from Dew.

While you can freely switch between classes, there are a few things to keep in mind:

You'll need to leave valuable items in your storage
There is a Gyldenblod fee you must pay (cost increases as you level up)
There is a cooldown before you can switch again (shorter cooldown at lower levels)
For a short time, there is a Reversion period to switch back for free
In the big scheme of things, you do have the freedom to swap classes but there are more barriers in comparison to a game like Elden Ring Nightreign.

Blackarrow (Bow, Javelin)


If you're an enjoyer of the scouting and sniping archetype, look no further than the 

Blackarrow
.

As the wielder of the Bow (and eventually the Javelin), 

Blackarrow
 can begin a fight from far away, often on high ground. 

Equipped with an arsenal of situational arrows and abilities like 

Shadow Step
, it's a great choice for players that want to outsmart and outposition their enemies.

See our Blackarrow guide here.

Mercenary (Hammer, Sword and Shield)


The 

Mercenary
 is the versatile melee fighter of Mistfall Hunter, wielding the Hammer or Sword and Shield. 

By using the Hammer, the 

Mercenary
 excels in forward movement with strong engage potential from abilities like 

Hammer Dash
. Once in the thick of combat, the Hammer provides AoE damage with the likes of 

Earthshaker
.

When wielding the Sword and Shield, the 

Mercenary
 has a more balanced approach, with gradual stacks building up for payoff with 

Stacked Slash
 on offense or timely parries with Perfect Block for parries.

Whether you want to be cautious and tactical or go all-out with massive bonks, you can't go wrong with 

Mercenary
 as an all-around choice.

See our Mercenary guide here.

Seer (Catalyst, Mace)


At first glance, the 

Seer
 seems like the clear choice for the cleric playstyle. With healing along with holy abiliites like 

Punishment Rune
 and 

Binding Rune
, it certainly fulfills that niche.

However, if you prefer the mace over the catalyst, you can embrace the "Blasphemer" archetype that favors surprisingly aggression.

You'll have to make that choice of which side to lean toward, you unfortunately cannot access both at once.

See our Seer guide here.

Shadowstrix (Dagger, Dual Blades)


Fans of the rogue playstyle will find their home in 

Shadowstrix
, the class with stealth and assassination as its focal point. 

Equipped with daggers, dual blades, poison, and even crows from abilities like 

Crow Storm
 and 

Poison Crow Mark
, they're the best choice for making quick work of an unsuspecting foe.

Naturally they make for potent flankers and duelists, but will need some tricks up their sleeve if they are caught in a drawn out teamfight.

See our Shadowstrix guide here.

Sorcerer (Staff)


Looking for magic wielder and don't want to bother with healing? 

Sorcerer
 provides a wide range of explosive firepower with the likes of 

Fire Bolt
 and 

Thunder Pierce
 but also has some defensive options like 

Forbidden Gate
 and 

Mistwalk
. It's one of the most complex classes but one of the most rewarding.

Similar to the 

Mercenary
, the 

Withered Knight
 is another melee class who can choose between going offensive with Greatsword or more defensive with Polearm and Shield.

If you want to play as a tank that can protect allies, the 

Withered Knight
 is the closest thing to it with abiliites like
