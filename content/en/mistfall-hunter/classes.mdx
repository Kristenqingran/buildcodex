Mistfall Hunter currently has six playable classes: Mercenary, Sorcerer, Blackarrow, Shadowstrix, Seer and Withered Knight. A class fixes your broad combat toolkit, but its weapon identities can change the range, rhythm and job you take into a hunt. This guide explains those differences without turning the roster into a tier list.

## How Classes Work in Mistfall Hunter

Each class has its own skills, talents and equipment restrictions. Official class material also presents two contrasting combat routes for most of the roster. Those routes are more than cosmetic weapon swaps: they can move a hunter from defense to pressure, support to offense, or close-range commitment to safer spacing.

Sorcerer is the important exception in how that idea should be described. Staff is the weapon identity; Stardust and Elemental are magic paths, not two separate weapon types. Blackarrow also has only Bow in the current playable kit. Bellring Games has announced Javelin as a future route, but it remains in development and should not be counted as playable.

The descriptions below combine official class identities with clearly separated editorial guidance about how those tools feel in a match. They explain roles and tradeoffs, not current balance rankings.

## All Mistfall Hunter Classes

| Class | Weapons or combat route | What shapes the playstyle |

| Mercenary | Sword and Shield; Hammer | Blocks, parries, sustained pressure or committed heavy attacks |

Mercenary is the most familiar-looking fighter in the roster, but its two routes ask for different habits. Sword and Shield gives you a block and parry-focused foundation, then lets the sword keep pressure on an opponent once an opening appears. It is readable at a glance, though official material notes that its defensive timing still has a high skill ceiling.

Hammer gives up some of that immediate protection for charged attacks, wider impact and stun pressure. Miss the timing and the commitment is easier to punish; connect cleanly and Mercenary can control a crowded melee instead of merely surviving it.

Start here if you want visible defensive answers and conventional melee spacing. Do not mistake “straightforward to understand” for effortless—the class still rewards learning enemy attack timings rather than holding forward.

Sorcerer fights through a Staff and two magic paths, Stardust and Elemental. It wants room to cast, a clear read on the fight and enough discipline not to spend every defensive option at once. The class can pressure an area from range, but poor positioning quickly removes that advantage.

Stardust and Elemental should be understood as different spell routes, not separate weapons. Their exact loadouts may change with balance updates; the stable identity is ranged magic, control and deliberate setup.

Official descriptions place Sorcerer among the higher-skill-ceiling classes. It suits players who enjoy predicting movement and shaping space more than trading repeated blows at point-blank range.

Blackarrow lives or dies by aim and distance. Bow attacks include charged shots and special projectiles, so the class can keep pressure on a target before melee range—but only if you create sightlines and land those shots. Elevation and retreat paths matter as much as raw aggression.

The Bow is Blackarrow's current playable weapon identity. Javelin has been officially announced as an additional route and remains in development; it is not part of the live playable weapon list at the time of this review.

Choose Blackarrow if manual aim is already part of the appeal. Players who panic when a target closes the gap will feel the class's limits much sooner than its theoretical range advantage.

</ClassSection>

<ClassSection name="Shadowstrix" weapons="Dagger · Dual Blades">

<YouTubeEmbed videoId="EqyOvVecYNY" title="Mistfall Hunter Class Reveal: Shadowstrix" />

Shadowstrix is built to choose when a melee exchange begins. Dagger and Dual Blades support quick attacks, movement and stealth-led approaches; the aim is to enter on favorable terms, force a reaction and reposition before the fight becomes a straight durability contest.

That mobility does not make the class forgiving. A mistimed entry leaves little room to absorb punishment, and extended frontal trades work against the assassin identity described by official material.

The class fits players who track openings and escape routes while attacking. If you prefer to stand your ground and correct mistakes through armor or blocking, Mercenary or Withered Knight offers clearer tools for that job.

</ClassSection>

<ClassSection name="Seer" weapons="Catalyst · Mace">

<YouTubeEmbed videoId="YMlTxBHJ53E" title="Mistfall Hunter Class Reveal: Seer" />

Seer has the sharpest role split of the six classes. Catalyst supports the Reverent side of the class: healing, protection, runes and tools that keep allies functioning. That route rewards awareness of the whole fight rather than attention to one target.

Mace shifts the class toward the Blasphemer's closer, more aggressive play. It gives Seer a way to contest space directly instead of remaining a back-line healer, but that does not turn the class into a heavy frontline fighter.

Pick Seer when managing teammates and changing the pace of an encounter sounds more interesting than chasing a damage rotation. Community guides discuss limitations around moving between its two routes, but the complete current restriction is not documented clearly enough here to state as an official rule.

</ClassSection>

<ClassSection name="Withered Knight" weapons="Greatsword · Polearm and Shield">

<YouTubeEmbed videoId="z_PA79P-hYo" title="Mistfall Hunter Class Reveal: Withered Knight" />

Withered Knight is heavy, deliberate and centered on Wither pressure. Greatsword commits to offensive swings that punish a clean opening. The pace is slower than Shadowstrix or Mercenary sword play, so choosing when to begin an attack matters.

Polearm and Shield leans into reach and protection. Official class material highlights defensive control and the ability to assist a teammate from range, giving the route a clearer team-facing job than simply dealing heavy melee damage.

This is a good fit for players who would rather control a lane and make fewer, weightier decisions. The tradeoff is recovery: rushed inputs and poorly chosen commitments are harder to erase with mobility.

</ClassSection>

## Can You Change Class in Mistfall Hunter?

**Yes.** Multiple current guides report that a character can change class through Dew at camp after the opening progression. The same character keeps its current level, while equipment tied to the old class may no longer be usable after the switch.

> **Community guide consensus · Checked August 24, 2026.** Bellring Games has not published a complete class-switching FAQ. Costs, timers, prerequisites and item restrictions can change, so the live confirmation screen is the final reference.

### How to Change Class

1. Progress until the relevant camp services are available.
2. Speak to Dew at camp and open the class-change option.
3. Store valuable or class-specific gear before continuing.
4. Review the Gyldenblod fee, cooldown and restrictions shown by the current game client.
5. Select the new class and confirm the change.

If the option is unavailable, check the requirements shown by Dew and remove incompatible carried or equipped items. Existing sources do not establish one universal prerequisite quest or level requirement, so this page does not invent one.

### What Carries Over When You Switch?

Current multi-source guides agree that the character's level remains. They do not provide a sufficiently reliable, current account of every skill, talent, saved build, quest and inventory state. Treat those details as unconfirmed and inspect your setup immediately after switching.

Class-restricted gear is the clearest practical concern: an item usable by the old class may not work on the new one. Storing valuable equipment first is a precaution, not a claim that the game always deletes carried items.

### Class Change Costs and Cooldowns

Current guides report a Gyldenblod cost, a cooldown before another change and a limited opportunity to revert. Exact prices and durations have not been verified as stable across versions or character states, so use the values displayed in game rather than a fixed number from an older guide.

## Which Class Should You Learn First?

Begin with a combat rhythm you can still read under pressure. A familiar defensive or ranged style usually makes it easier to learn enemy timing, extraction decisions and positioning before adding more technical mechanics.

This is a learning choice, not a strength ranking. For current solo, PvP, PvE and team recommendations, use the [Best Class & Tier List guide](/mistfall-hunter/guides/best-class/).

## Mistfall Hunter Classes FAQ

### How many classes are in Mistfall Hunter?

There are currently six playable classes: Mercenary, Sorcerer, Blackarrow, Shadowstrix, Seer and Withered Knight.

### Does every class have two weapons?

Most classes are presented with two weapon identities. Sorcerer uses a Staff with Stardust and Elemental magic paths, rather than two separate weapon types. Blackarrow currently plays with Bow; its announced Javelin route remains in development.

### Can you change class after character creation?

Yes. Multiple current guides report that the starting choice is not permanent and that Dew handles class changes at camp.
