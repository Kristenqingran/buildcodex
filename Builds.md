Guide
Overview

This build starts from a fact about the class most guides skip past: Black Arrow has the smallest dodge pool in the game and no built-in movement skill, in a game where everything else lunges, dashes, or teleports. Every affix and talent here is either compensating for that or converting it into a win condition.

The levels listed above are pre-drink. After the brew this lands at Valor 7, Focused 7, Aegis 5, Seeker 5, Elusive 5, Curse 3 — and those final numbers are the point, so read the allocation backwards from them. Valor and Focused are the damage engine and they get pushed all the way: Valor clears its defense penetration breakpoint at 5 and keeps climbing, and Focused is the real DPS knob on a class whose damage lives in fully charged shots — every level is more full draws per punish window. Aegis sits at 4 on gear deliberately, because the drink lands it exactly on the 5 breakpoint where it adds defense penetration and physical resistance on top of the raw mitigation. Not a point wasted, not a point short. Seeker and Elusive both cap at 5, and that's the survival budget in full: Elusive isn't a comfort pick, it's parity — every other class can take Elusive too, and they started with more dodges than you. Seeker is there because spacing is this class's health bar. Curse at 3 stretches your DoT durations so the pressure you apply keeps working while you reposition.

How to Play

The core loop is spacing. You win fights by controlling the distance at which they happen — establish a sightline, apply your debuff arrows early, and pressure with fully charged shots while the enemy solves the problem of reaching you. Focused makes those charge windows short; your job is making sure you're always in a position to use one. If an opponent is freely swinging at you in melee range, the build isn't broken — your positioning is. Reset it.

Quick tip that pays for the whole guide: dodging with an arrow equipped fires it instantly at zero charge. The damage is nothing; the debuff application is everything. You can open the engagement off a dash-shot around a corner without ever winning a charged-shot duel to get there.

Lasting Pain deserves its own mention — not as the gameplay loop, but as the enhancement that makes the loop forgiving. Every shot you land extends the duration of every debuff already on that target, so as long as you're maintaining natural pressure, your bleeds and poison stay live well past their listed durations. Stack Curse on top and your DoTs have real staying power through repositions and missed windows. What this buys you in practice is freedom: you don't have to choose between keeping DoTs up and playing your spacing game, because playing your spacing game is keeping DoTs up. Just know the inverse — go quiet for too long and the stack unravels, and you're rebuilding it from the opener.

Now the shroom, because it's the most misunderstood tool in the kit. Most players treat it like a damage skill that happens to make a cloud. It's not. It's furniture. Picture two guys on opposite sides of a table — nobody's throwing hands until someone deals with the table. It only activates on the ground, so stop thinking of it as something you aim at people and start thinking of it as something you build the fight around. Drop it between you and them: now they walk through it and eat the DoT, walk around it and hand you free draw time, or stand there staring at it — also fine, because a Black Arrow at range against a stationary target is not a fair fight.

And it earns its slot even when nobody walks in willingly. When you're playing around your shroom and someone commits onto you anyway, they're eating a guaranteed ~20% chunk from the spore DoT just for the privilege of opening on you. You're not trying to win that exchange outright — you're making sure every exchange costs them, and every arrow you land on the way out keeps that poison ticking longer than they budgeted for.

The part most people don't know: the shroom deploys through walls and through the ground. You do not need line of sight. Footsteps on the other side of a wall means the shroom is already waiting when they round the corner. Someone holding a floor above or below you — their floor is your ceiling, and the shroom doesn't care. This turns audio into damage. You're punishing positions you haven't seen yet.

When melee does reach you — and it will — dodge into the swing, not away from it. Dodging backwards feeds their lunge; dodging through them forces a turn, and a turning melee isn't swinging. Treat every melee like a boss with an animation to read. And when you're caught in a chain, don't panic-Breakaway: good melees do a one-two, pause, and hold their own gap-closer specifically to catch your escape. Eat the stagger, watch for the third hit, break on that.

In the trio you're also the eyes. Call what you see before you shoot at it — your frontline plays completely differently when they know where the threat is, and a called target that lives is worth more than an uncalled kill.

Tips & Tricks

The dash-shot fires your equipped arrow instantly at zero charge. Use it to apply debuffs on the move — it's the cheapest engage the class has.

With Lasting Pain, a landed quick shot beats a whiffed charged one every time — it deals damage and extends everything already ticking. Save the full draws for windows you actually own.

The shroom between you and them is a toll booth. The shroom pre-placed through a wall they haven't rounded yet is a mugging. Learn to do both.

Track your dodges like a resource. Elusive keeps you at parity with the rest of the roster, not ahead of it — fighting at zero dodges isn't a fight, it's a countdown.

Movement-speed slows do nothing against lunges. Don't expect Seeker or any slow effect to save you from a gap-closer — that's what the shroom, your dodge economy, and reading animations are for.

If a fight has gone longer than you wanted, disengage through the shroom and make them start over. Losing a trade isn't losing the fight — this build's whole identity is that it decides when round two starts.
